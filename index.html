The story

Some people wait to feel ready. the rest of us just show up.

Dragon has been Ballincollig's grappling home since 2014 — built on the idea that real jiu-jitsu should be taught patiently, in a room where a nervous first-timer and a competition black belt train side by side.

No egos on the door. Just structured, supervised classes that leave you sharper, safer and fitter every single week.

How you'll train

  1. Fundamentals that actually stick
  2. A room that has your back
  3. Tested where it counts

Every class is structured and led by a black belt. You'll drill the details that matter, slowly enough to learn them and often enough to keep them.

Students, shift-workers, parents and kids all share these mats. You'll leave your first session with new training partners — and a reason to come back.

Our competitors medal from local opens to the IBJJF Milan International Open. The exact game we teach you is pressure-tested at the highest level.

No egos. No spectators.
Just you, a gi, and the mats.

Programmes

A class for every age & level

Confidence, focus and anti-bullying skills through age-grouped, high-energy classes kids genuinely look forward to. Split into 5–8, 8–10 and 10–13 groups so nobody's out of their depth.

A dedicated space for teenagers to build discipline, fitness and resilience — and to burn off some energy the right way, alongside their own age group.

Structured Gi classes from day-one fundamentals through to intermediate. Turn up with zero experience — you'll be coached at your own pace, never thrown to the wolves.

Fast, dynamic grappling without the Gi — wrestling, scrambles and submissions for the modern grappler. A brilliant complement to your Gi game.

Sharpen your game for the podium in the competition class, then put it together in live open-mat rounds. Our athletes have medalled at the IBJJF Milan International Open.

Black belt · Head coach

Your coach

Piotr

founder & head coach

Piotr founded Dragon in 2014 and still leads the room himself. A competitive black belt, he coaches with the patience of a teacher and the eye of an athlete — breaking the art down so anyone can learn it, then building it back up for those chasing the podium.

First class or fiftieth fight, you're learning from someone who lives it every single day.
