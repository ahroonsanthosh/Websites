## 1. Benchmark observations: asset quality and loading

From direct inspection of the benchmark. Recorded as evidence of a standard.
Nothing here is to be copied.

**Environment and material realism.** Their surfaces are not flat fills. A
single HUD control is built from seven stacked passes (fill, colour-burn fill,
soft inset highlight, strong inset highlight, plus-lighter edge light, overlay
edge dark, darken inner glow) on top of a saturating backdrop blur. That is the
level of attention a single 40px button receives. The lesson is not "use glass";
it is that a material is built from many small correct observations, not one
effect.

**Environment lighting.** The page's base is an explicitly authored radial
gradient (seven stops, elliptical, sized in viewport units) that the WebGL
background pass then reproduces exactly, so the handover is invisible. The
gradient is treated as a lighting environment, with the DOM version existing
purely to cover the first paint.

**Asset quality.** Self-hosted OTF typefaces with `font-display: swap`; WebP for
UI imagery; SVG plus PNG plus Apple touch icon for brand marks; a JPG OG image
at declared 1920x1080; device-specific MP4 variants for video backdrops.

**Loading strategy.** A progress-driven counter to 99 with per-digit roll, masked
top and bottom. A body-level `webgl-loader-overlay` class gating the whole UI.
Per-stage loading state carried right into the navigation (each stage button has
its own spinner and disabled state). A full-screen white overlay with a spinner
for stage jumps. Video elements preloaded off-screen at 1px or 2px. Resource
hints (`dns-prefetch`, `preconnect`) for every third-party origin. Hashed,
code-split bundles.

**The principle to carry over:** loading is designed, progress is honest, and no
asset is fetched before the frame that needs it.

## 4. Classification key

Every asset is exactly one of:

- **DOWNLOAD**: an existing file retrieved from an external source or from
  Heloria's own current site.
- **BLENDER**: authored in Blender and exported.
- **PROCEDURAL**: generated by a build step or at runtime on the CPU.
- **GLSL**: exists only as shader source; no file is fetched at runtime.
- **THREE.JS GEOMETRY**: constructed from Three.js primitives in code.

Sources are restricted to Poly Haven, ambientCG, Fab / Megascans, Sketchfab, and
other sources **only after their licence has been checked and recorded here**.

**No asset is chosen because it looks impressive.** Each block states why the
asset exists, and what would be lost if it were deleted.

Listed so that these decisions are on record and do not get quietly reversed.
Each is either a banned anti-pattern (`CLAUDE.md` section 2) or a direct copy of
a benchmark interaction (`CLAUDE.md` section 1.1).

| Not created | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Particle systems, sprite sheets for particles | Random particles are banned. No particle asset will be added without a stated physical or narrative meaning. |
| Glass or frosted UI panel assets | Generic glass cards are banned. The benchmark's seven-layer glass is theirs. |

| Custom cursor sprite atlas | Would be a direct copy of a benchmark interaction, and a custom cursor carries no meaning for Heloria. |
| Loader counter digit artwork | The benchmark's counter is their interaction. Heloria's loading design must come from its own content, and progress must still be honest. |
| XP or score iconography | Belongs to the benchmark's product, not to a software company's site. |
| Background video assets | No content justifies video. Video would also consume most of the mobile transfer budget on its own. |

| Third-party model-library set dressing | Every model must justify its existence; none currently can beyond HL-BRD-003. |

## 7. Evaluation: `public/hdr/syferfontein_6d_clear_puresky_4k.exr`

**First, a correction of record:** this file is not in the repository. There is
no `public/` directory and no committed asset of any kind. The evaluation below
treats it as a named candidate.

**Verified provenance.** Syferfontein 6d Clear (Pure Sky), Poly Haven, **CC0**,
authors Greg Zaal with sky edits by Jarod Guest. It is a sky-only edit of
`syferfontein_6d_clear`. Published at 1K, 2K, 4K, 8K and 16K in EXR and HDR.
Metadata: 21 EVs, 5400K white balance. Described as a clear, low-contrast
sunrise: soft golden horizon, cool blue sky, calm light, subtle haze.

**The 4K EXR is 73.95 MB.** That single number decides the practical question.

### Verdict on the file as shipped

**Reject as a runtime asset, without qualification.** 73.95 MB is 12 times the
entire desktop transfer budget and 21 times the mobile one. It would also cost
roughly 100 MB of GPU memory once decoded to half-float, before any PMREM
target. No amount of tuning makes a 4K EXR a web asset.

### Verdict on the file as an authoring source

**Acceptable as a prototype, with three reservations.**

1. **Licensing is clean.** CC0 means no attribution obligation, though
   attribution is recorded here anyway as a matter of practice.
2. **The content may be wrong for the brand.** A golden sunrise with a cool blue
   sky is warm, calm and outdoors. The brand analysis found Heloria to be dark,
   near-monochrome and institutional. A clear-sky HDRI will push every metal
   surface towards warm gold and sky blue, which is a palette decision being
   made by an asset rather than by a designer. That is precisely the failure
   mode `CLAUDE.md` section 2 exists to prevent.
3. **A sky-only HDRI gives no ground bounce.** With no terrain in the
   environment, the underside of any object goes dead. A studio or interior
   environment, or a synthesised one, would light a single hero object better.

### Recommended path

1. Keep Syferfontein as the **prototype** lighting environment so that material
   work can begin, and mark it clearly as provisional in code.
2. **Download the 1K EXR, not the 4K.** For a prefiltered 256px environment, 1K
   is visually indistinguishable from 4K and is a fraction of the size. The 4K
   file offers nothing that survives prefiltering.
3. Never commit the EXR to the repository as a shipped asset. Either keep it out
   of `public/` entirely and process it in a build step, or commit only the
   derived KTX2 (HL-ENV-002).
4. Before the lighting decision is finalised, evaluate at least three
   alternatives against the brand: a dim studio environment, an overcast or
   twilight sky, and a fully synthesised environment generated in a shader. A
   synthesised environment is worth serious consideration here, because it
   removes the asset entirely, costs nothing to transfer, and can be tuned to
   Heloria's palette rather than to a photograph of South Africa.
5. Whatever wins, the shipped form is HL-ENV-002, never the EXR.

## 8. Staged loading plan

Priorities used throughout the register:

| Priority | Meaning | Budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Required before the first frame renders | 1.2 MB desktop, 900 KB mobile |
| P1 | Required before the first interaction | 400 KB |
| P2 | Fetched during an earlier stage, required by a later one | 900 KB |
| P3 | Lazy, fetched when its stage is next | remainder |

Rules:

- Nothing outside P0 may block the first frame.
- Every P2 asset is fetched at least one full stage before it is needed, so a
  stage transition never waits on the network.
- Progress reporting is honest: it reflects bytes actually received and GPU
  uploads actually completed, never a timed animation.
- GPU uploads are staged across frames. A single large upload in one frame is a
  visible stall even when the download was instant.
- Every material is prewarmed by an off-screen draw against the real environment
  before its stage begins. Shader compilation is the most common cause of a
  first-interaction stutter, and it is entirely avoidable.
- The mobile build selects mobile variants at load time. It does not download
  desktop assets and scale them down.

## 9. Open questions

These block finalisation of this specification. None of them may be answered by
guessing.

1. **The experience spec does not exist.** Every STAGE value here is
   provisional, and the asset list cannot be considered complete until the
   experience is defined. An experience with more than one physical object would
   add assets; one with none would remove HL-BRD-003 and HL-MAT-001.
2. **Typeface confirmation.** Is Heloria keeping Anybody and Martian Mono? Both
   licences must be verified, including embedding rights for MSDF generation,
   before HL-TYP-003 or HL-TYP-004 is produced.
3. **Kríos vector master.** Only a 140px raster is published. The vector is
   needed before HL-BRD-003 can be authored, and tracing the raster is not an
   acceptable substitute.
4. **Portrait originals.** Higher-resolution sources are needed, and the choice
   between DOM portraits and in-scene portraits decides whether HL-GEO-004,
   HL-SHD-006 and the KTX2 variants exist at all.
5. **What Kríos actually is.** The brand analysis found the product is never
   explained. If the new site explains it, that will require product imagery
   that does not exist yet and is not listed here.
6. **Lighting direction.** See section 7. The environment decision changes the
   palette of every material in the register.
